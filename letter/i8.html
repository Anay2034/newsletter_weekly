OpenAI Launches Dots, Always-On Agents Built on Astra
OpenAI launched Dots at DevDay on September 29, a personal AI assistant powered by GPT-6 Astra that keeps working on a user's goals in the background, even after they log off.

Key highlights of the launch:
  • Its Own Cloud Computer: Each dot gets its own cloud computer, browser and memory, and can reach more than 4,000 apps through OpenAI's plugin ecosystem, such as investigating a bug flagged in Slack.
  • Built for Organizations Too: OpenAI previewed specialist dots with their own identity and credentials, and is working with Microsoft to bring them under Agent 365 security controls so administrators can govern them.
  • Early Days: Dots start with Pro and Business Premium plans in eligible markets, one primary dot per user, and OpenAI warns they can still make mistakes, so consequential work should be reviewed.
Dots arrive weeks after Meta's Muse, escalating the race to build agents that act on users' behalf, and OpenAI plans to add more dots per user later.
AI Concept of the Week
Always-On Agent: An always-on agent is an AI assistant that keeps working toward your goals in the background, even when you are not chatting with it. With its own cloud computer, browser and memory, it can watch for alerts, chase bugs, update documents and reach thousands of apps by itself, like OpenAI's new Dots.

Pharma.AI Opens Its Tools to Outside AI Agents
Insilico Medicine said Model Context Protocol servers are now live across its Pharma.AI stack, letting outside AI agents connect directly to its biology, chemistry and antibody-design engines. Researchers can run multi-step discovery workflows, from target evaluation to antibody design, inside the AI tools they already use.
AI Learning Resource
Subagents: What is it? A subagent is a helper an AI agent creates mid-task to handle one scoped job in its own private workspace, then return a short summary and disappear. It lets agents like Dots split big projects into parallel pieces without getting overwhelmed or losing track.

Brokers Push Back Against IRDAI Reform Proposals
The Insurance Brokers Association of India, representing 798 licensed brokers, raised serious concerns over IRDAI's September 23 distribution paper, warning that commission caps could cost jobs and curb reach. It backs measures like claw-backs for proven mis-selling but objects that independent brokers face the lowest caps.

GST Council to Weigh Relief on Group Insurance
The GST Council is expected to consider on October 7 a proposal letting employers claim input tax credit on premiums for group health and life cover. Employers currently pay 18% GST on such policies with no credit in most cases, while individual policies have been exempt since September 2025.
