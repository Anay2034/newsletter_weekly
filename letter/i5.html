OpenAI Claims Breakthrough Proof of the Navier-Stokes Problem
OpenAI says an internal AI system has produced a proof for the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of mathematics' seven Millennium Prize Problems, unsolved for nearly 90 years.

Key highlights of the claim:
  • A Swarm of Agents: The proof came from roughly 10,000 coordinating AI agents, which arrived at a solution in about 88 hours before it was formalized in the verification language Lean.
  • What It Actually Shows: The proof demonstrates that 3D fluid flow can develop a "singularity," a point where speed shoots to infinity in finite time, a question mathematicians had puzzled over for decades.
  • A Credit Dispute: Mathematicians Tristan Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge say they had independently made major progress on the same problem using Anthropic and OpenAI tools, and question whether OpenAI drew on their unpublished work.
The Clay Mathematics Institute has not yet verified the proof, and mathematicians outside OpenAI are still reviewing the underlying reasoning before the result can be called confirmed.
AI Concept of the Week
AI Agent: An AI agent is a system that can plan, use tools and take multi-step actions on its own to complete a goal, rather than just answering a single question. This week's Navier-Stokes proof used roughly 10,000 agents working in parallel, each tackling a piece of the problem before their results were combined.

Google DeepMind Maps 9 Billion DNA Variants
Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome Atlas, a 1-petabyte resource with pre-computed predictions for all 9 billion possible single-letter DNA changes in the human genome. Each variant gets an AVI impact score, letting researchers instantly flag likely disease-linked mutations without running the model themselves.

AI Leaders Call for Slowing Down Frontier AI
Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay urging the industry to slow the pace of AI capability gains, warning of serious risks as models approach superintelligence. OpenAI's Sam Altman and xAI's Elon Musk publicly agreed, backing Anthropic's pledge to give independent evaluators permanent, employee-level access to its systems.
